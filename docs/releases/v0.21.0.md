# product-lifecycle-templates v0.21.0

Release date: 2026-10-22. This project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). This is a minor
release with one new bundle. Nothing was removed or renamed, so nothing you use today breaks.

## Summary

**You can now write a design brief.** It is the document a product team hands to designers once the problem
is chosen and one concept is picked, and before anyone specifies how that concept works. It is the library's
50th bundle, and the second for the `define` phase, after the problem statement.

## New

- **`design-brief`, in two sizes.** Lean asks for the challenge, who it is for and what the team learned, the
  chosen concept, and the handoff. Full adds the constraints on the design, the open questions for design, and
  the handoff's scope, time line and budget, each with its confidence stated. A worked example shows a
  filled, illustrative brief for a feature called Saved Views, written before that feature's PRD. The guide says when to write a creative brief,
  a specification or a Shape Up pitch instead, because all three are often called a design brief.
- **Every claim in the new bundle traces to a source.** Its research log records 32 sources. Each of its 228
  quotations was checked against the source page's raw text, not against a summary.
- **The catalog counts 209 document types.** Entry 209 is the design brief. Entry 208 is Marty Cagan's Product
  Opportunity Assessment, a ten-question go or no-go document, recorded as a candidate. It is not a bundle.

## Improved

- **`problem-statement` now covers a brand-new initiative.** Its guide shows where the blanks of Jeff Gothelf's
  Business Opportunity Statement go, and why its solution line stays out. The template moves to 0.1.2.
- **`project-brief` points you to the design brief** when what you are writing is a brief for a designer. Its
  version is unchanged.
- **The two decision records that v0.20.1 listed as unread are now confirmed.** The maintainer read and
  confirmed ADR 0079 (the design brief joins discovery-docs at define) and ADR 0080 (a fifth admission check,
  for a software audience).

## Fixed

- **Two counts in the documentation were wrong.** The install guide said the MCP server can fetch 97 template
  variants; it fetches 99. The README said twenty-four Tier-2 types were built; there are twenty-five.
- **The library's own build-cost reports no longer overcount.** A bundle's planning work was being billed to
  its build. This affects only the maintainer's records in `bundle-builds/`, not any template.

## Known issues

- **Nothing here makes any bundle better.** All 50 are `beta`, and no page calls one proven.
- **There is no opportunity statement bundle.** The maintainer parked it on 2026-10-22. If you need one, the
  problem statement's guide shows how to write that framing inside a problem statement.
- **Cagan's Product Opportunity Assessment is not researched yet.** Its boundary with `business-case` is
  untested, so do not treat entry 208 as a recommendation.
- **One question about the admission check from v0.20.1 is still open.** Nobody has ruled whether a source that
  does the same job under another name passes. It will be ruled the first time it changes an outcome.
- **A search filtered to one axis value can be cut short.** The MCP server's `search_templates` returns at
  most 8 candidates, and `deliver` and `utility` hold 10 bundles each and `develop` 9. `total_matched` in
  the response tells you when a list was cut.
