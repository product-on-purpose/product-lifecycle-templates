# product-lifecycle-templates v0.14.0

Release date: 2026-09-25. This project follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## Summary

**The library grows from 33 bundles to 35 with two new templates: an internal announcement and a change
request.** The internal announcement tells the people who did not do the work what a launch or change
means for them and what they must do. The change request asks for something already agreed, such as a
scope, a requirement or a schedule, to change, and records the decision on it.

Neither is proven to help anyone; both are researched, and both say where their sources disagree. The
change request carries its own argument too: a team that changes its backlog through its product owner
does not need one, and the bundle says so first.

## New

- **[`announcement-internal-comms`](../../templates/announcement-internal-comms/)** - a headline a reader
  can act on, what is changing and when, why it matters to the reader, what they must do and by when,
  what could go wrong, and where to learn more. **One size only**: the published templates it draws on are a
  subject line and a few sentences. It links to the release notes rather than
  repeating them; the notes record what changed, and the announcement says what that means.
- **[`change-request`](../../templates/change-request/)** - the agreed baseline being changed, the reason
  and what happens if the change is not made, the impact, and a decision from a stated set by one named
  decider. Lean for a team that needs a decision recorded; full adds the options considered, what the
  change leaves out, and what was updated afterwards. It covers change to an agreed project or product
  baseline; change to a running production system is a neighbouring practice it describes and does not
  template.

## Improved

- **Every quotation in both bundles' research logs was checked against the source's raw text**, not a
  retrieval tool's summary. The check caught a research agent overwriting a cached report with a broken
  extraction, which would otherwise have failed every quotation from it.
- **The last "nobody but the author has filled a template" lines are gone** from the plugin description,
  the usage-report form, the contributing guide and the explanation, how-to and tutorial pages. No page
  calls a bundle proven, and that rule is unchanged.
- **The `rfc` guide now says what ITIL's "RFC" means**: in IT service management the same letters stand for
  a request for change, and the guide sends that reader to the change request.

## Fixed

- **The catalog described the change request as IT service change** (a change advisory board, rollback, a
  Change Manager), while the library's own bug-report and issue-log bundles used it in the project sense.
  The entry now matches the bundle, and the internal announcement's entry no longer says "external".
- **The release-notes companion said it "completes" its family alongside three others.** The family has
  nine members.
- **The status page's MCP server figures were stale**: it said 58 template variants and 44 test assertions;
  the tree has 71 and 49.

## Known issues

- **Nothing here makes any bundle better.** All 35 are `beta`, and no page calls one proven.
- **The internal announcement is admitted on vendor and practitioner sources only.** No standards body
  publishes this document, and the bundle says so plainly.
- **One search can no longer list a whole phase.** The MCP server's `search_templates` returns at most 8
  candidates, and the `deliver` phase now holds 9. `total_matched` in the response tells you when a list
  was cut.
- **The shared worked examples disagree about release numbers.** The release-notes example lists sharing as
  shipped on 2026-06-30, while the launch checklist puts that launch in mid-July. Neither new example states
  a release number, and fixing the older ones is separate work.
- **Build cost is measured, not a rate.** The internal announcement cost $16.99 at API list rates and the
  change request $14.41 ([`bundle-builds/`](../../bundle-builds/INDEX.md)). The single-size bundle was the
  dearer of the two, so writing one template instead of two did not by itself make a build cheaper.
