# Scenario: Last-mile driver scheduling PRD brief

> **Input brief for a blind efficacy eval.** Every arm receives the ask and the facts below and
> nothing else. Judges never see this file. Authored from the document type description alone by an
> agent instructed not to open this library's templates, per
> [the eval protocol](../../docs/internal/eval-protocol.md) section 6.

## The ask

Dispatch has been building next week's driver schedules by hand in a spreadsheet for two years, and it is starting to break. Regional ops managers want something better before peak season hits, and they want it framed as a real proposal, not a wish list, so leadership can decide whether to fund it. Write up what we should build, who it is for, and why, with enough scope and success criteria that engineering can size it and design can start sketching without coming back to ask what we meant.

## What you know

- Dispatch currently builds next week's routes and shift assignments in a shared Google Sheet, rebuilt from scratch every Sunday night by one scheduler per region.
- The scheduler in the Denver region, Renata Okafor, says the Sunday rebuild takes her 3 to 4 hours and she has made the same double-booking mistake twice in the last month.
- Last quarter, 12 percent of scheduled shifts had to be manually reassigned on the day of delivery because of driver call-outs or route conflicts.
- Drivers use a separate mobile app (built in-house, called DriverGo) to view assigned routes for the day, but it does not connect to the scheduling spreadsheet, so updates require a manual re-push.
- Regional ops managers want any new scheduling tool to respect the union contract rule that no driver can be scheduled for more than 6 consecutive days without a rest day.
- The VP of Operations, Marcus Feld, has said publicly that he wants schedule-related customer delivery delays cut in half before the November to December peak season.
- There are 340 active delivery drivers across 9 regional hubs as of this quarter.
- The company also runs a separate long-haul trucking division with its own dispatch system, which is explicitly not part of this initiative.
- Finance requires any new internal tool request over 50000 dollars in build cost to go through a quarterly budget review board before engineering can start.
- Renata mentioned that drivers frequently text her directly to swap shifts informally, and those swaps often never make it back into the spreadsheet.
- The company's HR system already tracks driver certifications and vehicle class licenses, but nothing currently checks those against route assignments.
- A prior attempt to buy an off-the-shelf scheduling SaaS tool two years ago was cancelled after the vendor could not support the multi-hub, multi-union-contract rule structure the company needs.

## Retrieval probes

> Not shown to any writing arm. A judge answers these from the produced document alone.

| Question | Expected |
|---|---|
| Is the long-haul trucking division's dispatch system in scope for this effort? | No, it is explicitly out of scope; this effort covers last-mile delivery driver scheduling only. |
| What would make the team stop or reconsider this work? | A good document should name a stop condition, most plausibly failure to reduce schedule-driven delivery delays or failure to enforce the union 6-consecutive-day rest rule, tied to the peak season deadline; exact wording depends on the writer but must reference a concrete trigger, not a vague one. |
| Which dependency or integration carries the most risk to this effort succeeding? | The DriverGo mobile app integration (or lack of live sync between scheduling and DriverGo) and/or the HR system certification data feed, since neither currently talks to the scheduling process. |
| What is the guardrail or success metric leadership will judge this by, and by when? | Cutting schedule-related delivery delays in half, targeted before the November to December peak season, as stated by the VP of Operations. |
| Who decides whether the union rest-day rule (max 6 consecutive days) is correctly enforced, or who owns that constraint? | A good document should assign ownership, e.g. regional ops managers or a compliance/HR stakeholder, as the ones who validate the scheduling rules against the union contract; the exact name may vary but a real owner must be named. |
