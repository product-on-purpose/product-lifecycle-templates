# Scenario: Patient Intake Form Replacement - Input Brief

> **Input brief for a blind efficacy eval.** Every arm receives the ask and the facts below and
> nothing else. Judges never see this file. Authored from the document type description alone by an
> agent instructed not to open this library's templates, per
> [the eval protocol](../../docs/internal/eval-protocol.md) section 6.

## The ask

Our three-location clinic is still handing patients a clipboard and a pen for new-patient intake, and it's causing real problems: front desk staff are drowning in illegible handwriting, billing is eating rejected claims, and patients are annoyed at how long it takes before they even see anyone. I need someone to nail down what we're actually building to replace this, who it's for, and how we'll know it worked, because right now I've got the ops director, IT, compliance, and the medical director all pulling in slightly different directions and nobody's written down a single version of the plan. Talk to the facts I'm giving you, and where two people are saying different things, tell me plainly which one you're going with and why, don't just repeat both sides. I want this ready to hand to whoever ends up designing and building the replacement.

## What you know

- Average current intake time is 22 minutes per patient, according to front desk manager Denise Ruiz.
- Billing reports that about 40 percent of paper intake forms have at least one illegible or missing field, and each resulting claim resubmission costs the practice roughly 18 dollars in staff time.
- The practice has three current locations - Main St, Riverside, and Oak Park (opening in November) - and 6 front desk staff total across them, with Riverside currently short one FTE.
- A patient satisfaction survey of 214 respondents found 72 percent would prefer completing intake on a phone or tablet before arriving, but front desk staff report that patients 65 and older, roughly 35 percent of the patient base, struggle with tablets and complain to staff about it.
- The IT director describes the clinic's EHR system as Epic; a separate email from the practice administrator refers to 'our athenahealth system' when discussing the same integration.
- Approved budget for this fiscal year is 45,000 dollars; the vendor's quote for kiosk hardware across all three current locations came in at 61,000 dollars.
- The ops director wrote in a June email that there was 'no rush, Q1 next year is fine,' then more recently said in a meeting that the new process needs to be live before flu season, October 1, to reduce front desk overload during the clinic's busiest months.
- Compliance flagged that any new form must capture HIPAA consent and a photo of the patient's insurance card, and noted the state medical board requires records be retained for 7 years. Compliance also mentioned GDPR in passing, referencing an unrelated case involving a visiting researcher's records, though the clinic has no patients based in the EU.
- The medical director raised interest in voice-to-text symptom capture during a staff meeting as something worth exploring.
- Leadership said that if the Main St pilot causes intake time or patient complaint volume to rise above current levels in its first month, they will pause expansion to the other locations.
- The current no-show rate is 9 percent; one team member suggested in a meeting that the new intake process would probably cut no-shows in half, though no data supports that.
- Practice administrator Carla Nguyen has final sign-off on which locations go live and in what order; the medical director does not control rollout sequencing.
