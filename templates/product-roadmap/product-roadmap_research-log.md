## Honest framing (the through-line for the companion)

**1. Two literatures share this word, and conflating them would be a category error.** Technology
roadmapping has a documented, peer-reviewed, twenty-year academic tradition at Cambridge [1][2][3]. Software
product roadmaps have a much smaller and newer research thread [4][5][6][7]. Neither measures whether a
roadmap improves outcomes, and the first is not evidence for the second.

**2. Nobody has shown that any roadmap practice improves outcomes.** Searched arXiv, Semantic Scholar and
the general web for a causal link between roadmap format, cadence or confidence device and product or
business results. Nothing. This is a confirmed gap. **Two circulating statistics were found and deliberately
excluded** because they could not be traced to any study: a "60-70 percent less maintenance work" claim for
Now/Next/Later, and a "38 percent of best-performing companies" correlation. They appear nowhere in this
bundle, and their absence is the point.

**3. The practitioner consensus is real, and it is a consensus of prescription rather than measurement.**
Perri [37], Cagan [22], Bastow [12] and Torres [31] arrive independently at the same design principle:
**express less certainty, further out.** That convergence, from people who are not citing each other, is the
strongest thing this bundle can teach. It is still not evidence.

**4. The fight about dates is narrower than it looks.** Cagan names the two legitimate reasons management
wants roadmaps [21]; Torres says outright that her issue "is not with roadmaps altogether" [32]; and SAFe,
the most date-committed framework, commits only the current increment and labels the next two a forecast
[15]. The real disagreement is how far out commitment extends, and whether one document can serve both
internal engineering certainty and external sales.

**5. The origin is genuinely unresolved.** The industrial tradition is documented [1]. The modern software
roadmap has an independent first-person origin story [11]. No transmission chain between them was found, and
the encyclopedia article that would assert one does not [3]. Reported as **not verified**, not as absence.

## Claims flagged contested or time-bound

1. **Is a roadmap a promise?** [38] says no, [39] says yes, both published, both vendors. Teach the
   disagreement; do not resolve it by picking a side quietly.
2. **Do committed dates belong on a roadmap?** SAFe commits the current increment [15]; Torres [31] and
   ProdPad [51] say no firm dates. A framework-level split, not an error.
3. **Cadence has no single answer.** Fixed intervals [48] against trigger-based revision [28][30][47].
4. **The origin of the modern product roadmap is unresolved.** Industrial tradition documented [1]; software
   origin story independent [11]; no transmission chain found; the encyclopedia does not assert one [3].
5. **Now/Next/Later has two named creators** [9], not one as commonly reported, and its 2012 date is
   unverified against a period source. A competing vendor republishes it with no attribution at all [10].

## Notes for the companion

**The honest framing.** Open with the two-literatures problem and the absence of outcome evidence, then give
the reader the thing that is genuinely well-supported: four named practitioners, arguing independently,
converge on expressing less certainty further out. Convergence of prescription is worth teaching and is not
evidence, and the companion should say both.

**Load-bearing sections.** The confidence gradient is the one that cannot be dropped: it is what separates
every legal format here from a Gantt chart. "What is not on here" is second, because a roadmap without
exclusions is a wish list with dates.

**The sharpest teaching points.**
1. The word "roadmap" appears nowhere in the Scrum Guide [34]. Whatever your process says, this artifact is
   not part of it and you are choosing to have one.
2. "The roadmap shows the plan. The OKRs carry the commitment" [12] settles more arguments than any other
   sentence found.
3. A roadmap becomes a promise the moment a customer reads it, whatever the disclaimer says [41][40].
4. If a reader could build a Gantt chart from it, it stopped being a roadmap [42][51].
5. Both sides concede more than their reputations suggest [21][22][31][15].

**Formats to ship.** Three qualify under ADR 0028's rule. **Now/Next/Later** [9][12] is the default: three
confidence lanes, no false precision, and it works internally and externally without modification. **GO
Product Roadmap** [16][17] ships as an additional format for teams that need an explicit goal-to-metric grid
tied to releases. **The themes format** [25][26] ships for teams that need the strategic context, vision and
objectives in the same document. Five candidates were rejected: the timeline form has no named
product-management defender, the release plan and the release roadmap are a different artifact and a
relabel, the Kanban board has no named source, and the opportunity solution tree [24] and Cagan's
alternative [23] are excluded because **their own authors do not present them as roadmaps**.

**What the example must chain onto.** The Acme Analytics thread. The roadmap sits between the
`product-strategy` example and the Saved Views PRD, so it must contain the work that PRD describes.
