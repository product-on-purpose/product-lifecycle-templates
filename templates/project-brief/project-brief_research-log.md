# project-brief: research log

Research conducted 2026-09-29, building on an admission sweep run on 2026-09-27 while the spec was written. The
build's six-dimension fan-out covered the PRINCE2 canon, public-sector sources in both senses of the name, published
templates counted section by section, the neighbours that share the ground or the name, using the brief (the mandate,
the approval, what happens to it afterwards, how it fails), and the standing gap question. **33 sources are recorded
below, all fetched-and-verified.** Only `fetched-and-verified` sources are quoted anywhere in this bundle.

**Every quotation in this log was checked against the source's raw text**, not against the summary a retrieval tool
returns: pages as downloaded and PDFs through a text extractor. Every page first cached during this build was checked a
second time against a fresh download, so no quotation can pass against a copy an agent stored. The agents returned 149
quotations in their quotable fields. 118 passed as returned, and 14 more are verbatim apart from a rendering artifact:
a space before punctuation where a link closed, wiki link brackets, or a PDF line-end hyphen. The other 17 were
corrected rather than used. Six glossary definitions carried a headword label the agent had added, six section lists
were the agent's own composition, one glossary sentence was interleaved by a two-column layout, and three were a
trade-mark footer or a sentence with a stray symbol. Each was replaced by its verbatim parts or dropped. The structure
dimension returned its quotations inside its findings rather than its quotable fields; the phrases this log uses were
chosen from those findings and each was checked. Twenty-four more were added from the main loop's own reading of [6], [16]
and [33]. **272 quotations remain: 259 pass as normalized substrings, and 13 are verbatim apart from the rendering
artifacts above.**

## What the checks caught, and what was not read

**GovS 002 has no glossary entry for "project brief".** The spec recorded "project brief:" as a heading in [7]'s Annex
B whose definition could not be recovered. [7]'s text extracted cleanly and was read end to end, and the term is not
among Annex B's defined terms. The one passage naming a brief is in the body, among the senior responsible owner's
accountabilities: "the vision, justification and desired outcomes for the programme or project, together with any
strategic assumptions, are documented in a programme or project brief". [7] still shows the term in current UK
government use, in the body rather than the glossary. The spec needs a dated correction when this bundle lands.

**"Project management team structure" is verbatim on prince2.wiki, on a different page from the one the spec
checked.** The spec reported that the phrase failed a raw-text check against prince2.wiki and was "not confirmed as a
stated part of the brief by any source read". It is absent from [2], the project brief page, and present on [3], the
starting-up page: "Facilitates the creation of most of the project brief (project product description, roles &
responsibilities, scope, project management team structure, etc.)." [3] also names "roles & responsibilities" and "six
performance targets" among what is assembled into the brief. The Constraints and Who Should Be Involved section may
cite [3] for the brief's team structure, attributed to prince2.wiki.

**Two sections the spec labelled as the library's own position are sourced after all.** The spec wrote that Decision
Requested exists because the family contract obliges it, "not because a named source specifies this as a brief
component", and that Project Approach is "not elaborated in any source read". [3] makes the request itself the final
output of starting up ("Request to initiate the project: the final output of this process is to send a request to the
project board to initiate the project, which is to allow the project's first stage to start."). [6] states what
approval commits the approvers to, and [11], [15] and [33] each carry an approvals block in the document itself. For
the approach, [3] ("Select the project approach and assemble the project brief."), [15] ("7 Project Approach") and
[33] ("Proposed delivery approach", offering "Package/Custom/Enhancement/Outsourced") all name it. Both sections keep
their place, and their guidance can now cite sources. The contract obligation still explains why Decision Requested
names the document that takes over.

**[6] carries two lists, and the one the spec quotes is the start-up checklist.** The spec quoted BIS's "Objectives -
achievable and measurable (SMART)" and "Scope - what in and what's out" as the brief's checklist. Both are items in the
start-up checklist, introduced by "By the end of project start-up all interested parties should be satisfied that the
following aspects of the project are clearly defined and understood:", which has fourteen items. The brief's own
contents list ("The Project Brief will cover all the key areas of the project giving details of:") has eleven bullets
as printed, one of which reads "Stakeholders Resources" with no separator; this log counts it as printed and does not
guess at a lost comma. The research agent returned only the contents list, and the start-up checklist was added from
the main loop's own reading.

**Both BIS lists, and the AXELOS definition itself, name time and cost.** The spec's section design has no home for
either. See "The gap question's result" in the notes below, which records where this build puts them.

**The AXELOS glossary does not state its edition.** [1] states "v.1.1" and a 2012 copyright, and only its file name
says 2009. The companion describes it as AXELOS's PRINCE2 glossary, v1.1, and says the 2009 attribution rests on the
file name.

**Tasmania prepares its brief after approval.** [10]: "The Project Manager prepares a Project Brief once the project has
been approved by the Project Sponsor and/or Project Steering Committee." See contested item 4.

**What was not read.** The PRINCE2 6th (2017) and 7th (2023) edition manuals are behind a subscription; [31] is a
practitioner's account of the 7th edition's brief, not the manual. AXELOS's PRINCE2 Agile Appendix A.19 is unreachable,
because its host no longer resolves and no archive exists; the spec records this as a closed gap. The published APM Body
of Knowledge 7th edition is members-only: [22] is the file circulated for the 2025 consultation, and [23], the sample
extract, does not carry [22]'s sentence. BIS's own downloadable brief template, which [6] points to ("For further
guidance on the contents for each section please refer to the downloadable template."), was not located. Oxford
Brookes' template file behind [17] did not resolve. Templates from the University of Edinburgh, UCL, the University of
Manchester, Tunbridge Wells and Bedford Borough returned a 403, a Cloudflare interstitial, a login page or a redirect.
The Irish government guideline [32] cites (DPER CWMF GN 1.2) was not read. No PMBOK 7 body was read, and [29], the PMI
Lexicon, lacks the term.

## The admission record

**[ADR 0048](../../docs/internal/decisions/0048-one-named-source-clears-the-admission-test.md)'s bar is met in the sense
this bundle builds.** [15] is a council's own blank project brief template, filed in a public committee paper, and [33]
is a vendor's blank template. [1] is the standards body's own definition ("created pre-project during the Starting up
a Project process"), and [6] is a UK government guide that describes the brief's contents and use. [2], [3], [10],
[17], [30] and [31] describe the practice. [8], [9] and [16] publish documents under the same name in the heavyweight
sense, and [32] describes one; see below.

**Two senses share the name, and this bundle builds one.** The lightweight sense is short, written before commitment,
and superseded by initiation documentation: [1] ("It is superseded by the Project Initiation Documentation and not
maintained."), [2], [6], [31]. The heavyweight sense is large, gated and revised across the project's life: [8] ("The
Project Brief is in four Parts"), [9] ("The project brief is an iterative document that progressively reflects the
state of the project with each update"), and [32] (a Preliminary Project Brief developed into "a Detailed Project
Brief, which is a deliverable required within Phase 5"). [16] sits between them: a council's "Detailed brief" with a
SWOT block and funding rows. The companion names the heavyweight sense as a different document under the same name, so
a reader who arrived looking for it is told plainly that it exists and is not this one.

**Licences decide what this bundle may adapt.** [6] and [7] are Crown copyright under the Open Government Licence ("You
may re-use this information (not including logos) free of charge in any format or medium, under the terms of the Open
Government Licence."), so BIS's wording may be adapted with attribution, and **[6] is the adaptable source for template
guidance text**. [1] is all rights reserved ("Reproduction of this material requires the permission of AXELOS
Limited"): quote it briefly, never adapt it. [2] states "PRINCE2 wiki is provided by EMPII Group with a Creative Commons
Attribution license.", but it uses AXELOS's trade marks under a permission of unstated scope, so this bundle describes
its structure in its own words rather than adapting its text. The other sources state no licence on the pages read, or
reserve rights: quote them, never adapt them.

**The family is `discovery-docs`, `phase: discover`, by
[ADR 0063](../../docs/internal/decisions/0063-project-brief-reopens-discovery-docs.md).** ADR 0063 carries a strain:
[6] calls approval "the official start of the project", and the family is for documents written before anyone commits
to building. The sources read here support ADR 0063's reading without settling it. [6]'s own list of what approvers
confirm ends with a commitment to planning: approvers are willing to provide the time and resources "needed to plan
the project in detail and to produce the Project Initiation Document (PID)." [18] says the
approved brief "does not give authorization to complete the project activities", and [2] says the board's decision is
"whether to authorise the initiation stage". What approval commits to is planning. That approval commits to planning
and not to building remains this library's reading of the family boundary, as ADR 0063 states it.

## Claims flagged contested or time-bound

1. **What the brief contains.** [1] names four things: "the purpose, cost, time and performance requirements, and
   constraints". [2] names seven: "background, objectives, scope, constraints, risks, stakeholders, and outline business
   case". [3] adds "roles & responsibilities", "six performance targets", the approach and "project management team
   structure". [6]'s contents list has eleven bullets and its start-up checklist fourteen items. The templates differ as
   widely: [11] ten sections, [12] seven, [13] five core elements, [14] four key elements (with "there's no
   one-size-fits-all project brief template or style."), [15] ten numbered sections. **No composition is standard.**
   Each list is attributed to its source by name, and [2]'s list is never presented as what "PRINCE2" or "AXELOS" says.

2. **Whether the outline business case sits inside the brief.** [1]'s start-up entry lists "the outline Business Case,
   Project Brief and Initiation Stage Plan" as three outputs. [2] names "outline business case" in the brief's content,
   [18] says "in PRINCE2, the creation of the business case (in outline form) is part of the project brief", and [15]
   gives it a section of its own ("10 Outline Business Case"). [6] lists "Desired benefits and who will realise them"
   and "Outline estimates of time and cost". The bundle carries an outline justification that defers the full
   comparison to `business-case`.

3. **What approval authorizes, and whether it is the project's start.** [6]: "Approval of the Project Brief is the
   official start of the project". [2] and [18] limit it to initiation, and [22] makes the rationale "the starting point
   for governance to approve the initial investment, so the project can be further defined." In the heavyweight sense,
   [32]'s Gateway 1 considers "the availability of project funding". The lightweight sources agree that approval
   authorizes planning. They differ on whether to call that the project's start.

4. **Before approval, or after.** [1] places the brief "pre-project", and in [6] its approval starts the project.
   [10] prepares it "once the project has been approved", to document "what is to occur in the INITIATION Phase". [10]
   uses the name for a different point in the sequence; the bundle follows [1], [2] and [6].

5. **Brief and charter: four camps.** (i) Sequenced, with narrower authority: [18] ("It does not give authorization to
   complete the project activities."), [20] ("the appetizer before the main course (your Project Charter and PID)").
   (ii) Different purposes, no stated sequence: [14] ("A project charter formally authorizes a project and grants the
   project manager authority to allocate resources. A project brief summarizes key details to align the team."). (iii)
   The same document, lighter: [19] ("A project brief is a simplified project charter."), and [5] ("the Project Charter
   serves a similar purpose as the project brief"). (iv) Different names for one rationale: [22] ("Different names for
   this rationale in common usage include 'problem statement', 'project brief' and 'project charter'."). [29], the PMI
   Lexicon, does not name a project brief at all. The bundle adopts camp (i) as a POSITION, by ADR 0063, not as a
   finding.

6. **Retired, or kept alive.** [1] ("superseded by the Project Initiation Documentation and not maintained"), [2] ("the
   project brief is no longer used in project management activities") and [31] ("The Project Brief is not used after
   the Initiation stage.") retire it. [9] resubmits it "at regular and meaningful intervals throughout the life-cycle of
   the project", and [32] keeps it under change control ("Any proposed changes in subsequent Phases to the Project Brief
   shall only be implemented with the approval of the NTA."). The lightweight sense retires and the heavyweight sense
   lives on; the bundle builds the first.

7. **Length.** [19]: "It should be a single page long". [14]: "as short as a paragraph" up to a page, and "A project
   brief should be one page or less." [11]: "often one to two pages". [13]: "1 to 2 pages", and "should rarely exceed
   three pages". [2]: "short, focused". [6] scales it to the project: "For small projects this will be a very short
   document often with only a few sentences for each section". No source measured length against outcome.

8. **Small projects: skip the brief, or stop at it.** [6] lets a short, well-defined, certain project go "straight on to
   production of the Project Initiation Document ... without the intermediate step of the Project Brief." [30] says
   "many small projects never need a PID at all", [20] says "Smaller projects may suffice with a Project Brief", and
   [10] lets the brief "be used instead of a Project Business Plan for small projects". The sources give opposite advice
   for the same case.

9. **Measurable objectives: required or encouraged.** [2]: "Make sure the project brief follows the SMART criteria". [6]:
   "Objectives - achievable and measurable (SMART)". [14]: "Aim to set SMART objectives". [15] qualifies them: "wherever
   possible". [12]: "Define 2 to 4 measurable outcomes before work begins." [13]: "measurable or observable goals". [11]:
   "Write success in terms someone else could verify". [16] carries no measurability wording.

10. **The brief as a pitch.** [20] frames the brief as a thirty-second elevator pitch. [30] frames it as permission:
    "A brief that is filled in but never approved is a wish list, not a mandate". This bundle's boundary with a proposal
    or pitch (a pitch sells a decision not yet made; a brief records a mandate and asks for authority to start) is the
    library's own framing, as the spec says, and the companion labels it so.

11. **Unmeasured claims, barred as fact.** No source measured whether writing a brief changes a project's outcome. The
    using-the-brief dimension searched for such evidence and found none: [30] and [31] make no such claim, and general
    project-success studies do not isolate the brief. [12]'s figures are unmeasured and disagree with each other on one
    page. [11]'s "The explicit out-of-scope list is what actually stops scope creep", [12]'s "This is the section that
    most teams skip", [13]'s "often leads to misaligned expectations, scope creep, and rework" and [14]'s "helps prevent
    scope creep" are assertions. The bundle may say that several sources recommend an explicit exclusions list. It must
    not say, as fact, that an exclusions list reduces scope creep or that most teams skip one.

## Notes for the companion

**The honest framing.** A project brief turns a mandate into a request for authority to start. BIS puts it in one
sentence: "In essence, the Project Brief says why the project is needed, what it must achieve and who should be
involved." ([6]). It is written before commitment ("pre-project", [1]), for whoever owns the decision ("It is primarily
written for the project board", [2]), and approved to authorize planning rather than delivery ([2], [6], [18]). It is
superseded once initiation documentation exists ([1], [2], [31]). PRINCE2 named it ([1]). The UK government's BIS
guide describes it in text the Open Government Licence lets this bundle adapt ([6]), and a council publishes a blank
one ([15]). Past that core the sources disagree: on what goes in it, on whether the business case sits inside it, on
whether a charter is the same document, on how long it runs, and on whether it retires or lives on. The same name also
covers a heavyweight, iterative public-sector document ([8], [9], [32]) that this bundle does not build. No source
measured whether writing a brief changes a project's outcome, and the companion says so.

**The evidentiary spine, section by section.** The template's sections are the spec's; the sourcing below is this
log's, and it replaces the spec's sourcing where the two differ.

- **Background and Mandate.** The trigger: [6] ("Start-up is triggered when a Senior Responsible Owner (SRO)
  agrees/decides to take responsibility for a new initiative that might best be run as a project."; "The trigger may
  come from business planning, an external driver (e.g. EU legislation, compliance requirement) or identification of a
  significant problem that cannot be dealt with as a matter of routine."). The mandate: [1] ("An external product
  generated by the authority commissioning the project that forms the trigger for Starting up a Project."), [6] ("often
  as simple as an email"), [31] ("can vary in form from a verbal instruction to a well-defined and justified project
  definition"), [2] ("It is based on the project mandate, which may contain limited or detailed information depending
  on the source."). Why now: [6]'s start-up item "Background - why does this project need to be done and why now?".
- **Objectives.** [6] ("Objectives - achievable and measurable (SMART)"; "Project objectives define what a project must
  achieve for it to be judged to be complete and successful and hence able to be closed."), [2], [30] ("Avoid vague
  verbs like improve, optimise or streamline, because nobody can tell when they are done."), [11], [12], [15].
- **Scope and Exclusions.** [6] ("Scope - what in and what's out"), [15] ("3 Project Scope and Exclusions", with its
  prompt "What is outside the remit of the project?"), [12] ("Section 4: Out of Scope"), [11] ("Scope: in and out"),
  [13] ("Scope boundaries clarify what is included in the project and what is explicitly excluded."), [30] (the
  out-of-scope box, and "Writing the exclusions down once saves a dozen awkward conversations later."). **Deliverables
  belong here, as what is in scope:** [6] lists "Deliverables" and "Deliverables/outcomes", [15] "10.1 Project
  Deliverables", [16] "Expected outcomes and key deliverables".
- **Outline Business Justification.** [2] ("outline business case"), [18], [15] ("10 Outline Business Case"), [6] ("The
  reasons for the project"; "Desired benefits and who will realise them"; "Outline estimates of time and cost"), [33]
  ("Value of performing this proposed project"; "Impact of not performing this proposed project"). Its boundary is
  the shipped `business-case` bundle's (`business-case_guide.md:17`): the full comparison of options, including doing
  nothing, belongs to the business case, and this section says that a business case will bring it to a named approval.
  It carries the brief's outline estimate of time and cost; see the gap note below.
- **Constraints and Who Should Be Involved.** [6] ("who should be involved"; "Constraints that must be taken into
  consideration during the project"; "Stakeholders (internal and external)"; "Dependencies on other
  projects/activities/decisions"), [1] ("constraints"), [3] (the brief's "project management team structure", per
  prince2.wiki), [12] (three decision roles: "Project owner", "Decision-makers", "Contributors"), [33] ("Project
  influences and dependencies"). **Known risks and assumptions belong here too, as a line or two each:** [2]'s list
  names "risks", [3] describes the brief as "including the scope, objectives, risks, and approach", [6] lists
  "Assumptions" and "Risks" among the contents and "Any known risks" in the start-up checklist, and [13] pairs "Scope
  boundaries and assumptions". A formal risk-scoring table stays out, as the spec's heavyweight exclusion says.
- **Decision Requested.** [2] ("It is used by the project board to make the first key decision: whether to authorise
  the initiation stage."), [3] (the request to initiate), [6] (what approvers confirm, quoted in the admission record),
  [30] ("The sponsor’s sign-off at the bottom is what turns the draft into approval to start."), [11] ("Get it approved
  by the decider, out loud."), [15] (the approvals and authority blocks), [33] ("PROJECT BRIEF DOCUMENT APPROVAL"). The
  family contract's obligation is also met here: the section names the document that takes over.
- **Project Approach** (full). [3], [15], [33]. [33]'s options ("Package/Custom/Enhancement/Outsourced") are one
  vendor's vocabulary; the guidance may offer build, buy or a mix and attribute the idea to these sources, not to
  PRINCE2 generally.
- **Relationship to Other Documents** (full). Supersession by initiation documentation: [1], [2], [4], [31]. [30]: a PID
  "is the detailed plan - scope, schedule, governance, roles and risk - that is maintained as the project runs", and
  "many small projects never need a PID at all". The charter camps: [18], [19], [20], [22] (contested item 5). The
  product brief: [24]. The creative and design briefs: [25], [26], [27]. The canvas: [28]. The heavyweight sense: [8],
  [9], [32]. The business case: the `business-case` bundle's own boundary text. Neither a project charter nor a PID is
  built in this library, and the section says so.

**The gap question's result, and what this build does with it.** Dimension 6 found one qualifying source, [33], and it
records a null result for RACI charts, a definition of done, escalation paths, communication plans, and sustainability
or equality impact: no published brief read shows any of them. Each candidate below went through
[decision procedure 12](../../docs/internal/decision-procedures.md#12-a-property-or-section-is-proposed-for-a-template).

- **Outline estimates of time and cost: adopted, inside Outline Business Justification.** E1, sourced: [1]'s definition
  names "cost, time and performance requirements", [6] lists "Outline estimates of time and cost" among the brief's
  contents and "Estimated timescale" and "Estimates for resources required" in the start-up checklist, and [33] asks for
  preliminary cost and benefit totals. Budget or cost appears in [11], [12], [15] and [16], and a timeline in every
  template counted except [15]. [14] dissents: its brief drops the budget ("your project brief only needs four of these:
  project objectives, timeline, target audience, and project scope"). E3, homed: in the outline justification, because
  it is the outline the business case later costs option by option. It is not a section of its own, and it is not a
  cost breakdown, which the spec's out-of-scope list keeps out as the heavyweight sense. E4, sized: one or two lines,
  so it fits lean.
- **What happens if nothing is done: adopted as an ASK in Background and Mandate, not an element.** [33]'s "Impact of
  not performing this proposed project" sits beside [6]'s "why now?". The comparison against doing nothing stays in the
  business case.
- **Dependencies: folded into Constraints and Who Should Be Involved.** Sourced by [6] and [33], and homed there.
- **A recorded approval: folded into Decision Requested**, as the approver's name and the answer. Sourced by [11], [15]
  and [33].
- **Lessons learned from similar projects: a guide rubric candidate, not an element.** One source ([6]'s start-up
  checklist).
- **Priority rating and a discretionary-or-not classifier: not adopted.** One vendor's governance fields ([33]).
- **Target audience: not adopted.** [14]'s element is a marketing audience. The brief's reader is its approver ([2]).

**What the bundle must not say.**

- "PRINCE2 says" or "AXELOS says" for [2]'s composition list. Only [1]'s wording is AXELOS's.
- That GovS 002 defines "project brief".
- That PMI or PMBOK names a project brief ([29] does not).
- Any figure from [12], or that a brief, or an exclusions list, reduces scope creep or improves outcomes.
- That the brief is a pitch or a proposal, except as the library's labelled POSITION.
- The heavyweight sense ([8], [9], [32]) as the document this bundle builds.
- That leaving out options analysis is what makes a brief lightweight. [15], a short council template, carries "9
  Project Options" and "10 Outline Business Case" as sections of its own. This bundle leaves the comparison of options
  to `business-case` by the library's own boundary (`business-case_guide.md:17`), and the companion states that as a
  POSITION, not as a fact about how lightweight briefs are written.
- [6]'s approval list as one quotation. The PDF leaves a bullet mid-sentence ("time and resources · needed"); quote the
  sub-clause "needed to plan the project in detail and to produce the Project Initiation Document (PID)" instead.
- AXELOS's wording, adapted into template guidance. [1] is all rights reserved; [6] is the adaptable source.
- Any gendered pronoun for a source author. Use the author's name.

**GOOD and WEAK illustrations use their own scenario.** Every GOOD and WEAK example in the templates' guidance comments
uses a scenario unrelated to the worked example: not Acme Analytics, not the Question-First Entry initiative, and none
of Dana Okoro, Priya Nair or Meridian Freight.

**The example.** A project brief for the **Question-First Entry and Modelling Defaults** initiative at Acme Analytics,
dated **2026-01-16**. That is two days after the product vision (2026-01-14), after the user persona (2026-01-05), and
four days before the business case (2026-01-20). It must precede what it leads to and must not cite any later document
as existing. It reads these facts from its siblings and invents none of them:

- **People.** The executive and sponsor is Dana Okoro, VP Product (`business-case_example.md:4`). The project manager
  is Priya Nair, PM, Reporting (`user-persona_example.md:6`).
- **Background.** Acme's own telemetry, which predates the business case: "only 31 percent build a second dashboard
  view within 14 days of signup" and "revenue per new account has been flat for five quarters"
  (`business-case_example.md:34`, `:41`).
- **Mandate.** "the flat revenue-per-account trend surfaced at the December leadership review and finance asked for a
  funding decision" (`business-case_example.md:52-53`).
- **Who it serves.** The Recurring Analyst (`user-persona_example.md:43-47`).
- **Decision requested.** Dana Okoro authorizes proceeding to a funding decision at the leadership review on
  2026-01-26 (`business-case_example.md:115`). The document that takes over is that business case, arriving four days
  later.

It must not contain:

1. Any staged-case vocabulary. `business-case_example.md:5` says "Acme has no staged case model (no SOC/OBC/FBC)".
2. The Reporting Platform Modernization program or Marta Reyes, which are mid-2026 facts of a later thread.
3. The business case's own analysis: its benefit range, NPV, IRR, payback or ROI (`business-case_example.md:75-99`).
   **This log tightens the spec on one point: the example must not cite the prototype's 71 percent result either.** No
   sibling dates the prototype, and every document that reports the figure is dated after this brief.
4. An options decision. It must not name or choose OPT-3; comparing options is the business case's job.
5. A project charter, a PID or any initiation documentation cited as existing.

Its outline estimate of time and cost, if it gives one, is labelled illustrative. It must be an envelope that the
business case's own figures fall inside (read its Costs table: a $420,000 build over 14 engineer-weeks across two
pods), and it must not name an option. The opening "Worked example" blockquote may mention later documents.
