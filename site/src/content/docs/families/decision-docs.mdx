Four document types that sit around a single decision, separated by **what they do to it**.
That is the whole discriminator, and it is what keeps the family from collapsing into one
type with four names.

| Type | What it does to the decision |
|---|---|
| **Spike Report** | Investigates the question that *precedes* it |
| **RFC** | *Proposes* it |
| **ADR** | *Records* it, once taken |
| **SDD** | *Describes the design* that implements it |

<Aside type="note" title="Membership is decided by a written contract, not by resemblance">
Each family carries a contract with an **exclusion clause** - the sentence written to test a
candidate. When a family's role inventory and its exclusion clause disagree about whether
something belongs, the exclusion clause governs. That is a recorded decision, not a
convention, and it was made because a candidate type once satisfied one and failed the other.
</Aside>

## What admission costs

A type does not join this family because it seems to fit. It has to clear an admission test:
**at least one named source** that publishes the document as a document. One type was refused
on its own evidence after a full research pass found none - and the refusal was recorded with
the same rigor as an acceptance, because a library that only documents its inclusions is
telling you half of what it knows.
